How to paste lyrics
  • Song Shape never downloads lyrics or songs. You load your own audio file and paste the lyrics yourself.
  • Original lyrics (optional): the words of the reference song, if you have them. Without them, place words by clicking, or skip step 7 and work from the shape only.
  • Your replacement lyrics: the words you want sung with the same delivery (Step 1), then any new sections in that style (Step 2).
  • One sung line per line. Leave a blank line between sections. Section names are optional — a line such as [Chorus] only marks where a section starts.
  • Any [bracket] tags and clear performance cues in parentheses such as (whispered) are ignored. Song Shape writes its own tags: [Verse] / [Chorus] (a block that repeats is the chorus) and a [Delivery: …] line built from the measured rise and fall. Unclear parentheses such as (oh oh) stay as sung backing words and are flagged.
  • Leave out titles, credits, chords and notes; they would be read as lyric lines.
  • Leave out standalone dashes (-, –, —) between words: they break word timing, and Song Shape will ask you to remove them. Hyphenated words such as well-known are fine.

        Step 1 · Map my lyrics to the reference performance

        Your lyrics, sung the way the reference singer sings: paste them one line per sung line, a blank line between sections, matching the original line by line (ending included). You get a ready-to-paste sheet with section and delivery tags.

        Step 2 · Extend in that style

        Now add your own extra sections in the style you just mapped. Click Start from my Step 1 lyrics, then type each new section where you want it (anywhere, also between existing sections) with a line such as [New Verse], [New Chorus], [New Bridge] or just [New] above it. Each new section inherits the tags of the same section type already mapped (the nearest one before it). Your Step 1 sections keep their mapping, including the ending.

        Lift on: new sections keep their source section's tags but step up one notch (higher register, longer holds, more power), most of all on the final line (long hold on the last word, lifted ending). The sheet and the JSON mark what changed.

        How sections are matched, and naming your own

        The reference sections (verse, chorus, bridge, vocalise, outro) come from the reference lyric tags, or are detected from repeated lines, wordless lines and long instrumental breaks. Your sections are matched to them in order. Each gets a [Vocal style: …] tag (pace, register, intensity, held notes, contour) plus per-line delivery tags. If your first section starts with a tag such as [Verse], you are naming sections yourself: give each section a tag ([Verse], [Chorus], [Bridge], [Outro], [New Verse]…); a blank line with no tag after it stays inside the same section (a phrasing gap), and your names are kept. A section left over with no original section to follow is treated as new.

        The vocal performance JSON (Step 1 or Step 2) holds, per section, line and word: the reference timing, relative pitch contour, register, holds, dynamics and phrasing, your lyric and the reference line it maps to; new sections are marked new with their source section. Hand it to a reasoning AI to help with tag mapping. No audio is inside. Schema: docs/vocal_json_schema.md.

        Your lyric words are never changed. Pace is syllables per second of singing (no BPM is measured). Register and intensity come from the measured reference contour. Tags and style prompts describe the sound; no artist is named.

        Lyric sheet

        Suno-style generators do not guarantee that bracketed cues will be followed. Tags are suggestions, not promised control. The lead identity is written once; harmony cues appear only where you marked backing harmony under that lead.

        Advanced: pitch tracker (step 8 by hand)

        Step 8 runs this for you. Use it by hand to re-measure part of the song. It listens to the audio (the vocal stem if one is open) and draws the green measured line, which is separate from your own strokes. A whole song takes a few seconds; a 15–20 s loop is near-instant.

        The vocal stem sets the baseline to the midpoint of its confident lead range in semitones (2nd–98th percentiles, excluding harmony). Low through high register is relative to that range. Without a stem this field can still set a fixed baseline; otherwise the draft uses the median pitch.

        No GPU or AI needed. v0.2.11 cleans the line: octave jumps are folded, short spikes removed, short gaps bridged and stray dots dropped. It is still a measurement, not a verified match for how the singer delivers the line.

        AI context file

        Every AI request starts with the Song Shape context (SONG_SHAPE_AI_CONTEXT.md) so the model knows the tool, the stages, the data and the Suno tag rules. You can load your own version for this tab.

        AI learns from your hand traces

        Pick finished hand-traced projects as examples. The AI drafts the trace window by window (about 12 s, grouped by lyric line) from the locally measured pitch contour plus your placed word timings and syllable counts, then the windows are stitched together; with no examples it drafts from that evidence alone. AI strokes appear dashed and tagged ai_drafted_trace / needs_review: correct them by ear (delete, or redraw in Trace mode). Examples stay in this tab only and are never saved or exported. Nothing is sent until you click Draft.

          For many songs without hand work, use the command line: node cli/songshape.js ai-batch <folder> -o <out> --endpoint … --model … (see README).

            About Song Shape

            Song Shape is designed to track a single lead singer performance. Harmonies, backing vocals and choir parts are not tracked; harmony labels are hints found in the vocal stem, not facts. Unplaced words may be hiding in a harmony section.

            The original Song Shape has no tracking, analytics, accounts or data collection. Use the official repository as the verified source; copies from elsewhere may have been modified.
